Seven choices the build made for you; each is one line to change.** (a) *Cash and fraud alerts go to the owner alone.* The Controller used to get them too; SPEC §4 says owner only, and the people being checked may be the Controller. (b) *Quiet hours silence urgent alerts as well*, even a critical one: it is written to the feed, the manager and storekeeper are still pushed (they are staff on duty, not under your quiet hours), and the "[URGENT]" reminder to you waits until the quiet hours end. (c) *What counts as "now"* is five things: stock about to run out, a gap in the stock, a cash drawer outside tolerance, a suspicious transaction, a locked account. (g) The limit counts pushes, not alerts: one held for the night or for the limit does not use a push up.

10. **Four choices in the M-Pesa check that a real till may overrule (S6.2).** (a) *A sale's time is when its bill was closed* (`completed_at`), else when it was opened; the till has no "paid at" time, and a dine-in guest pays at the end of the meal. ### S6.1 The M-Pesa statement comes in — done

**Plan.** Goal: an owner uploads the CSV they export from M-Pesa, and the payments on it are stored, safely and once. What the matching does with them is S6.2. Test first: `tests/test_s61_mpesa_statement.py` (101 tests), `tests/fixtures/mpesa/`. Files: `parsers/mpesa_csv.py` (behind a `StatementParser` interface), `owner/mpesa_statement.py` (reading, de-duplicating, storing: also what the demo restaurant will use), `routers/owner_mpesa.py` (`POST /owner/mpesa/statement`, `GET /owner/mpesa/statements`, `DELETE /owner/mpesa/statements/{id}`), models `MpesaStatementUpload` / `MpesaStatementLine`, migration 055, `middleware/body_limit.py`, `demo/wipe.py`.

**How it works.**
- *The parser* is tolerant about what varies between exports (a preamble; other words for the columns; other column orders; UTF-8 with or without a BOM, or Windows-1252; five date formats, always day-first; "1,250.00" or "KES 1,250") and strict about what the matching needs. A row needs a receipt number, a completion time, a status of completed and one amount; one that does not is **left out and listed**, with its row number and why ("the payment is 'failed', not completed", "receipt SJE4K2LJ77 appears twice in this file", "the time 'not a time' is not one we can read"). Times in the file are Nairobi time and are stored as naive UTC; money is integer cents, and "1e3", "NaN", half a cent and Arabic digits are refused rather than read. A file that is not a statement, is empty, binary, over 5 MB or over 20,000 rows is refused in the owner's words and stores nothing. A second format is one class registered in `PARSERS`.
- *The upload* is idempotent on M-Pesa's own receipt number, per restaurant: the same file twice, or two statements whose periods overlap, add only what is new, and a file that brings nothing stores no empty upload. An upload can be undone (`DELETE`), which takes its payments with it. Owners, managers and controllers (the accountant) may; every other role 403, anonymous 401; another tenant's upload id is a 404, and the same receipt at two restaurants is two payments.
- *The body limit.* The whole API refused any request body over 1 MB ("a JSON API with no legitimate large-upload endpoint"). A month of a busy till is a few MB, so `LARGER_BODY_PATHS` lets exactly this path carry 6 MB (a 5 MB file and the form's framing); a test pins that no other path gets it. Note the body is read before the caller is authenticated, so those 6 MB are allowed to anyone who can reach the URL; the existing per-IP rate limit is the only other guard.

**What the build found.**
1. **Fuzzing the parser with 20,000 random byte strings found a path that raised the csv module's own error** (a single field over 128 KB, and a NUL byte). Both now say, in words, that the file is not a well-formed CSV / not text. A fuzz test stays in the suite.
2. `demo/wipe.py` keeps a list of every table with a `restaurant_id` and a test fails when one is missing; the two new tables are in it (lines before the uploads that own them).
3. Mutation checks, each of which fails a test when put back: a delete / list / count not scoped to the restaurant, de-duplication across restaurants, an empty upload stored, failed payments counted, Nairobi time not converted, a repeated receipt kept, money-in and money-out on one row, money not strictly digits, a waiter allowed, no room for a statement, every path given the larger limit, no size limit in the parser.
4. The engine this feeds, `mpesa_match`, is still not built: the code map now looks for its matcher (`owner.mpesa_match`), not for the parser, so it keeps saying so until S6.2.

**How it works.**
- *Now.* `alert_levels.push_decision` is asked for each owner in the fan-out: a "now" event is pushed unless it is the owner's quiet hours (Nairobi time, may wrap midnight, same hour twice means never quiet) or they have already had `urgent_alerts_per_day` pushes since midnight in Nairobi. Held alerts and the escalation reminder do not use up the limit. Only owners are under this: a manager or a storekeeper is pushed at any hour.

No sales and nothing held means no digest; a second run the same day sends nothing; one owner's failure does not stop the others; it is not pushed inside the quiet hours.

**Plan.** Goal: the four reports an owner reads on a schedule — daily (did today go to plan), weekly (how did the week perform), monthly (the full Profit & Loss, with the accountant's file), yearly (the trends) — built from the same blocks as Home so they cannot disagree, with no section left "planned". **What each report holds.** Daily: sales · best sellers · cash count check (today's counts only) · things to check · stock today. Weekly: sales · best sellers · best and weakest dishes · price ideas · missing stock · wages · cash count check · things to check · next week · kitchen speed · bookings · suppliers. Each section carries its state, the days it covers ("Today", "The last 7 days", "October 2026 so far", "The last 12 months"), one sentence made from its own rows, the rows, and what it rests on. **Decisions.**
1. **The monthly report is the calendar month, not a rolling 30 days.** Before, its sales and best sellers covered the last 30 days while its Profit & Loss covered the calendar month: two windows in one report. The section's `window` says which, rather than a weekly report implying a week.
3. **The "Why?" detail is where an engine's own words may appear.** The price ideas carry the pricing engine's reason ("Selling 1.3x above baseline (EWM-smoothed)"); it is in `why`, behind a fold, not in the visible note (SPEC §3.4).

**What the build found.**
1. **The "ran out" evidence cannot come from sales.** My first version flagged a dish that stopped selling while the restaurant kept trading. On the demo's holiday Tuesday it flagged the *Burger* and missed the planted Chips: at the demo's volumes (Chips ≈ 1.6 plates a day) a dish going quiet late in the day is chance, not a signal, and a Poisson test ("selling none would happen by chance less than 1 time in 20") then cleared the Chips too. The reliable evidence is the **stock ledger**: replay it from the start of the day (today's quantity less everything recorded since), and the shelf fell below one serving at 18:59. That is what the answer now uses, and it is checked against the sales (no plate may sell after the shelf was empty, or the ledger is wrong and nothing is claimed), against the restaurant still trading afterwards, and against *something having been recorded that day*. Four mutation checks pin those guards; each failed a test when removed.
2. **The planted Chips stock-out explains almost none of the bad day.** The holiday Tuesday sold KES 3,830 against a usual KES 10,809; the missing Chips are about KES 15. The answer says both numbers and does not call the stock-out "the reason". The spec's example reads as if the chips were a main driver; at the demo's volumes they are not. Worth knowing before the client video says otherwise.
3. **Ledger sign convention.** The real app records a downward stock adjustment as an `OUT` of a positive quantity; the demo generator writes a *negative `ADJUST`*. The replay reads both (an `ADJUST` is added as stored), but the generator should write what the app writes.
4. **Thresholds.** The four tests of the WHY form (60 minutes of trading after the shelf emptied; fewer than 75% of a usual day's staff hours; cancelled sales at least twice usual and at least 3) are heuristics, not owner rules; they live together in `answer_why.WHY` and are *shown to the owner in the answer's assumptions*. §0.3 says every threshold is a setting. **Plan (S3.1).** Goal: the data the money layer will compute from — what the owner spent, what recurs, what expires — recorded honestly. - **Money.** `amount_cents` integer in the database; the API takes and returns `amount_kes` (a decimal, at most 2 places, > 0, ≤ KES 100,000,000) — `0.1 + 0.2` can never reach a ledger.
- **Dates.** An expense has `paid_on`, a **Nairobi calendar date** (the spec says `paid_at`; an accounting entry is a day, not an instant, and a date column cannot drift across the UTC boundary the way D17 did). Not more than one day ahead, not older than five years.
- **Who.** The spec says "manager/accountant roles"; there is no accountant role. The Controller ("the financial role", directive 015) is the accountant; owner/admin, manager and controller may read and write; every other staff role gets 403. One restaurant = one branch: everything is scoped to the caller's active restaurant, which `get_restaurant_or_none` resolves inside the caller's own tenant, so there is no id to forge; an id in the URL that is not this restaurant's is a 404 (not a 403 — nothing is confirmed to exist).
- **Nothing is deleted.** An expense is *voided* (who, when, why) and drops out of lists and totals; bills and licences are switched off. A ledger that can lose rows cannot be audited.
- **Bills have a due rule**, not a date: monthly on day N (clamped to the month's length), weekly on a weekday, yearly on a month + day. `POST /bills/{id}/pay` records the expense and links it to the bill, which is how S3.3 knows a bill is settled.
